# HASS Energy

Home Assistant custom integration (domain `hass_energy`) that plans battery usage from Nordpool prices and PV forecasts. It's distributed via HACS.

## Status
Rebuilt from scratch and grown step by step with the user. Currently there is a config flow that picks a Nord Pool entry and area (one entry allowed), then the inverter's sensors and the battery and prices to plan with (also under Configure), a sensor with the average hourly home consumption over the last 10 days, read from the Energy dashboard's recorder statistics (which also give the average for each hour of the day), a sensor with the battery plan's command now, and a "HASS Energy" sidebar panel with a chart of today's and tomorrow's Nord Pool spot prices, with solar, battery, grid and home power on one shared kW scale, the battery's state of charge as a filled line, and a battery plan strip under it. Up to now, solar, battery, grid, home and state of charge are measured from those sensors (a line whose sensors aren't set has no past). After now, solar is the forecast summed from the forecasts linked to the Energy dashboard's solar sources (e.g. Forecast.Solar); battery, grid, home, state of charge and the plan strip come from the backend's battery plan (`planner.py`), which is empty until the battery is set. The integration doesn't control the inverter itself: the user's automations act on the command sensor. Don't add features beyond the step being worked on.

## Commands
- `uv sync`: install (Python 3.14, Home Assistant pinned via pytest-homeassistant-custom-component)
- `uv run pytest`: tests
- `scripts/lint`: ruff format + fix
- `scripts/develop`: dev HA on localhost:8123 (config in `config/`)
- `HA_TOKEN=… uv run python scripts/seed_demo.py`: seed the running dev HA with 14 days of grid/solar/battery statistics and set them as the Energy dashboard sources, linking any Forecast.Solar entries to the solar source

## Structure (`custom_components/hass_energy/`)
- `__init__.py`: entry setup/unload only. Creates the coordinator, stores it in `entry.runtime_data`, starts the hourly refresh and the solar and measured tracking.
- `config_flow.py`: Nord Pool entry and area (`data`), then one form of sections (`SECTIONS`, key → selector): the inverter's sensors (battery, power, lifetime energy), then the battery (capacity, lowest/highest charge, maximum powers, round-trip efficiency, wear cost) and prices (import fee, VAT, export compensation) the plan is made with. All optional, flattened into `options` (a number 0 is kept); `PLAN_DEFAULTS` in `const.py` fills the plan settings that aren't set. A battery that can't be planned isn't saved (`_errors`: only some of `BATTERY_REQUIRED` set, or lowest charge not below highest). The options flow (`OptionsFlowWithReload`) shows the same form and reloads the entry.
- `coordinator.py`: `HassEnergyCoordinator` gathers all data into `models.EnergyData`, refreshing hourly at `REFRESH_MINUTE`. Also defines the typed `HassEnergyConfigEntry`. It makes the battery plan (`_async_plan`: options → `Battery`/`Tariff`, `EnergyData` → `slots`, `plan_battery` in the executor) after each refresh, after a solar refresh, and at the first measured refresh of a quarter it hasn't planned in yet (or, without a plan, at any measured refresh), from the charge now, one at a time; a replan is sent in the same update as the data that triggered it. A forecast within `REPLAN_CUTOFF` of a quarter's end waits for the next replan. The plan is empty without the battery's capacity and maximum powers, its charge, prices from now on or the consumption by hour; any failure while planning only logs and leaves it empty. Once the entry unloads (`async_shutdown`), nothing more is sent, so a plan still running then can't reach the panel after a reload.
- `models.py`: plain dataclasses passed from the coordinator to the entities and the panel, including the plan: `PlanPeriod` (command and its power, solar, home, battery and grid kW signed as on the panel, charge at start and end, cost, activity) with the `Command` and `Activity` enums, whose values the frontend uses too.
- `nordpool.py`: adapter over the official Nord Pool integration's `nordpool.get_prices_for_date` service (prices per MWh in, `PricePeriod`s in currency/kWh out). Delivery days are CET days: the coordinator fetches the ones covering the local today and tomorrow, caches each published day, and doesn't ask for tomorrow before 12:45 CET. A failed fetch only leaves prices out. The integration is an `after_dependency`, and tests register a fake service (`tests/common.py`).
- `solar.py`: adapter over the solar forecasts linked to the Energy dashboard's solar sources (`config_entry_solar_forecast`), read through the same energy platforms as the dashboard (each integration's `energy.py` → `async_get_solar_forecast`, Wh per period; Forecast.Solar keys a period by its end, others by its start). Linked entries are summed into hourly `SolarPeriod`s in kW, 0 at night on the days they cover. A failing or unloaded forecast is only left out. The coordinator re-reads just the forecast when the Energy dashboard is saved (`async_setup_solar` turns the manager's listener into a signal), when a linked entry changes state, or when its coordinator updates. Tests add fake `forecast_solar`/`other_forecast` platforms (`tests/common.py`).
- `consumption.py`: home consumption from the Energy dashboard's statistics (grid import + solar + battery discharge − export − charge), in one hourly query: the average over `CONSUMPTION_WINDOW` (the sensor) and the average in each local hour of the day (the plan's forecast of the home; an hour with no data gets the average). Like `measured.py`, it reads one row more before the window, and `hourly_change` leaves out hours where a meter's row doesn't follow the one before it (its first row, or the first after a gap), since their change isn't that hour's use.
- `planner.py`: the battery planner, pure Python (no `homeassistant` imports). `slots` turns prices (through `Tariff`: buy = (spot + fee) × (1 + VAT), sell = spot + compensation), the solar forecast and the consumption by hour into 15-minute `Slot`s from now to the end of the known prices (stepped in UTC). `plan_battery` is dynamic programming over stored energy (`SOE_LEVELS`, spanning the limits and the charge now if it's outside them, so energy above the highest charge still counts) whose choices are the inverter commands (`_candidates`: auto, hold, then charge/discharge at `POWER_STEPS` powers, in tie-break order). The cost to go is worked out exactly; going forwards, a command must beat an earlier one by `TIE_TOLERANCE` kWh at the median buy price, so the plan doesn't switch commands on interpolation noise. `_step` (`simulate`) is the one place a command's flows are worked out; both passes use it, so the plan's flows are the ones it priced. The cost is grid energy at buy/sell prices plus wear per kWh charged; energy left at the end is worth `terminal_value` (median buy price × efficiency, but no more than buying it back at the cheapest price, so it never buys just to end full). `Battery` and `Slot` work out their derived values (`min_soe`, `max_soe`, `efficiency` each way, `hours`) once, as the DP reads them for every state and choice. `activity` derives what the user sees from the command and flows. About 0.12 s for a 44-hour horizon on a Mac.
- `measured.py`: measured power and state of charge from the sensors in the options. Each quantity (`QUANTITIES`) has a lifetime kWh total and a power sensor; each `Measured` line (`LINES`) is a signed sum of quantities, and a quantity with no sensor counts as 0 (so a net grid meter set as the grid power gives net grid power). 15-minute periods since the local midnight come from the recorder's 5-minute statistics in one query: kW from the total's `change`, or the power sensor's `mean` when no total is set. Each line's `now` comes from the power sensors' current states (`with_live_values`). The coordinator re-reads the periods at `MEASURED_REFRESH_SECOND` past every fifth minute, and the values now on state changes, debounced by `LIVE_COOLDOWN` and sent to the panel alone (entities aren't updated for them). A failed read keeps what was measured before. Growth across a gap in the 5-minute rows is left out. Partial updates (solar, measured) go through `_async_set_partial_data`, which doesn't touch the last refresh's success.
- `websocket.py`: websocket commands the panel reads through. `hass_energy/subscribe_data` sends `{prices, solar, measured, plan}` on subscribe and after every coordinator update (via a dispatcher signal, so it survives entry reloads). Each measured line is `{periods, now}`, or null when its sensors aren't set. `plan` is the plan's periods from now on, every `PlanPeriod` field (empty without a plan). Between those, changed values now arrive on their own as `{now}` (`SIGNAL_LIVE_UPDATED`).
- Domain logic goes in its own module with no entity code (e.g. `consumption.py`, `planner.py`). Recorder queries run in the recorder executor, batched into one query.
- `entity.py`: `HassEnergyEntity` base (device info, unique id from the description key).
- `sensor.py`: description-driven. A new sensor is a `HassEnergySensorEntityDescription` with a `value_fn` over `EnergyData`. `battery_command` is an enum sensor of the `Command`s for automations: `EnergyData.command`, the plan's first period, so it changes when a replan lands (about 30 seconds into the quarter), and `auto` without a plan, so an inverter is never left in a forced mode. For the same reason it stays available when a refresh fails, the coordinator drops a plan still expired at `PLAN_EXPIRY_SECOND`, and its entity ID is fixed, whatever the language.
- `panel.py`: serves the `frontend/*.js` modules (a view registered once in `async_setup`) and adds/removes the sidebar panel with the entry. HA's service worker serves cached copies of these first, so the panel URL carries `?v=<hash of the files>` and the panel passes it on to its imports. Any JS change gets new URLs once the entry reloads.
- `frontend/hass-energy-panel.js`: the panel, a plain web component with no build step. HA sets `hass`, `narrow`, `route` and `panel` on it. Its UI strings live under `common` in `translations/en.json` and load through `frontend/get_translations`.
- `frontend/energy-chart.js`: the chart component. It owns the plot, the marker, hover and keys, and draws a list of `lines` into it, each on its own scale around the middle. Pointing near a line lights it up along its whole length and grays out the others. The first line is the main one (time range, key steps, drawn on top). Lines are plain `ChartLine` objects (`frontend/chart-line.js`: the base class, `hourlyAverages` and the shared path helpers, e.g. `drawSplitPath` and `lineStrokeStyle` for solid-then-dashed lines), not elements: each brings its scale, colours, readout and drawing (there's no legend: the readouts name the lines, and the strip names the activity next to the pointer). Every line gets 15-minute `periods` and draws their hourly averages (`hours`), while its readout shows the 15-minute value at the marker; there's no resolution switch. `frontend/price-line.js`: prices centred on their average, a thin solid line in the theme's text colour, handles negative prices; its readout sits on a row of its own above the others, which have fixed widths so changing values never rewrap them. `frontend/power-line.js`: `PowerLine`, the shared base for power lines (kW centred on zero, so it can go negative; one colour; dashed where estimated; optional `directions` so the readout says which way it flows), and `PowerScale`, the one kW scale the panel's power lines share so their sizes compare. Signs: up is a source feeding the home, down is power leaving it (solar generating, battery discharging, grid importing and home use are positive; battery charging and grid exporting negative), so solar + battery + grid = home. `frontend/solar-line.js`, `battery-line.js`, `grid-line.js` and `home-line.js` each subclass it with their name, colour, string prefix and directions. `frontend/soc-line.js`: the battery's state of charge, a `ChartLine` on a fixed 0–100 % scale from the plot's baseline (not centred), with a gradient fill under it; it's last in the panel's `lines` so the fill sits under the others. A new line is a new `ChartLine` subclass added to the panel's `lines`, not a change to the chart. `frontend/plan-strip.js`: battery plan strip component under it, which shows the activity's name next to the pointer over the bar (kept for a few seconds after a tap) and lists every run in its aria-label, with the hour axis when shown (the chart's `axis` is then off). They're siblings over the same time range: the chart fires `marker-change` and the strip `hover-time`, and the panel passes each to the other. `frontend/timeline.js`: what they share (time scale, hour/day axis, past dimming, SVG helper, common styles). The panel subscribes to `hass_energy/subscribe_data` and splits every line at the end of the quarter that holds now (re-split every minute): the measured periods before it, that quarter with the value now, then the estimates (the solar forecast, trimmed to start at the split; hourly, so its readout is hourly too; battery, grid and home power and the charge halfway through each period from the plan's periods after the split). The strip shows the whole plan, from now on. It never reads other integrations directly.
- `const.py`: domain, logger and tunables.
- Tests mirror the modules (`tests/test_<module>.py`). Shared data and helpers go in `tests/common.py`, fixtures in `tests/conftest.py`.

## Architecture rules
The user wants a clean, scalable structure, so keep to these rules as the integration grows:
- **Platform files are named after their platform and hold all of its entities**: `sensor.py`, `binary_sensor.py`, `select.py`, etc. HA imports them by filename when `__init__.py` forwards the platform, so never rename them (e.g. no `consumption_sensor.py`). Add a sensor by adding a description to `SENSORS` (plus a field on `EnergyData` and a name in `translations/en.json`), not a new class. If the descriptions get long, move them into modules imported by `sensor.py`.
- **One module per concern; keep it flat.** HA expects platform files at the package root and hassfest/reviewers expect the flat style. Use a subpackage only when one concern outgrows one file.
- **Data flows one way:** HA data → domain logic → coordinator → `EnergyData` → entities. Entities only read `coordinator.data`. They never fetch, compute or schedule.
- **Keep domain logic free of HA where it matters.** The battery planner must be pure Python (no `homeassistant` imports): plain dataclasses in, a schedule out. Then it can be unit-tested without HA and swapped out later. The coordinator adapts HA data to it.
- **Separate data access from logic once there are several sources.** `consumption.py` mixes the recorder query with the averaging for now, which is fine with one source. When Nordpool/PV reads arrive, add a thin adapters layer that fetches HA data into plain dataclasses, with pure functions on top. Don't add layers (DTOs, repositories) before they solve a real problem.
- **HA idioms:** typed `entry.runtime_data` (no `hass.data[DOMAIN]`), `_attr_has_entity_name` + `translation_key` for names, unique IDs `{entry_id}_{key}`, `PARALLEL_UPDATES = 0` on coordinator platforms. No blocking I/O on the event loop: recorder queries go through `get_instance(hass).async_add_executor_job`, everything else through `hass.async_add_executor_job`.
- **Efficiency:** batch recorder queries (one `statistics_during_period` for all IDs), refresh on a schedule aligned to when data changes rather than polling, and share one coordinator refresh across all entities.

## Conventions
- Bump `version` in `manifest.json` for releases. Tag `v<version>` to release.
- UI strings go in `translations/en.json`, never in code, and in the same keys in `translations/sv.json` (Swedish; `tests/test_translations.py` checks every language has English's keys and placeholders). Frontend strings live under `common`, which HA only allows flat, so each component's keys share a prefix (`panel_`, `energy_chart_`, `price_line_`, `solar_line_`, `battery_line_`, `grid_line_`, `home_line_`, `soc_line_`, `plan_strip_`, `timeline_`) and each component reads its own through a `_t(key)` helper. Times and ranges are formatted with `Intl` (e.g. `formatRange` in `timeline.js`), not by joining strings.
- Keep `hacs.json` → `homeassistant` in step with the minimum HA version actually tested.
